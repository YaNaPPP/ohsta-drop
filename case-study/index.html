[ Case study ]   Landing page · UX/UI · Motion · Build

Built to
sell out.

OHSTA is a concept streetwear label that drops one collection a month: 300 hand-reworked suits, gone in a day. Its landing page is a business tool before it is a picture — it has one job, to turn a Friday’s attention into reservations. Everything on it either earns attention, explains the offer, builds trust, or leads to Reserve.

[ Three decisions ]   The ones that matter most

[ 01 ]

The wow never blocks the button.

The first version made visitors sit through a 13-second AI clip before they could act. The film now plays as eleven real frames shown as CCTV captures — hard cuts, a ticking camera clock, nothing that morphs. The page frame and the drop facts are there from the first capture, and Reserve is live after about five seconds. Anyone who saw the intro in the last week skips it: the finished wall sprays back on in two.

[ 02 ]

The brand idea is the interaction.

OHSTA takes the most obedient garment ever made and breaks its rules. So the page never explains that — it does it. The manifesto fills in as you scroll, then FIT IN gets sprayed out and STAND OUT goes over it. Every product card names the dress-code rule its piece breaks and strikes it out as the card arrives. The same five marks — strike, stencil, splotch, drip, tear — carry all the motion.

[ 03 ]

Every road leads to Reserve.

There is one action on this page. Six things lead to it: the O of INCOMING, the product tags, “Reserve →” on every card, the cursor turning into a Reserve bubble over each photo, a sticky bar with a live countdown, and the form itself. Reserving is free and takes ten seconds; the list gets the shop link 30 minutes early, which gives people a reason to sign up now instead of on Friday.

[ The full story ]   Nine chapters — the page as an argument, the buyer’s five questions, mobile and the system

01 / 09

Built to sell out

OHSTA (a concept brand) drops one collection a month: 300 hand-reworked suits, gone in a day. The landing page has one job — turn a Friday’s attention into reservations.

02 / 09

The page is an argument

Every section answers the next question in a buyer’s head: Why should I stop scrolling? What is this? What exactly can I get? Is it worth the price? How do I get one?

03 / 09

01 · Attention

CCTV captures instead of a cutscene. A clock from page load shows when each layer lands: the facts first, INCOMING at 3.7 s, Reserve at about 5 s.

04 / 09

02 · Understanding

One sentence carries the brand. It fills in as you read; FIT IN is sprayed out and STAND OUT goes over it. The offer follows in three numbers — 300 · 10 · 0 — and the zero counts down.

05 / 09

03 · Desire

Scarcity you can count: 10 designs × 30 pieces = 300. The rail moves sideways like a shop window, leans with your scroll speed, and every card sprays out the rule it breaks.

06 / 09

04 · Trust

The handwork is the reason for the price, so the page shows it. Found → Ripped → Reworn on one pinned screen; RIPPED tears in two and the detail scraps get ripped off the wall.

07 / 09

05 · Action

Six ways in, one destination. Free, no card, ten seconds. Early link at 17:30. The countdown always points to the next Friday 18:00, so the page never shows an expired drop.

08 / 09

Mobile

Not a shrunken desktop. On phones the CCTV captures play on a monitor, then blue paint floods the screen; every section is re-composed for 390 px.

09 / 09

The system

Two typefaces (PP Monument Extended for the shout, PP Neue Montreal for the facts), four colours, five marks from spray paint. One wall, one vocabulary.
